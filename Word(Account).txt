Account for (To explain / To make up a proportion).
It means to take full responsibility and give a clear explanation rather than just telling a story, or to claim credit for a certain percentage of a contribution.
Example:
1. How do you account for the drop in sales?
2. The new strategy accounts for 30% increase in traffic.
3. He could not account for his sudden absence yesterday.
4. Rent accounts for half of my monthly salary.
5. How do you account for the missing money?
6. International students account for 20% of the class.
7. I need you to account for every penny you spent on this trip.
8. Bad weather accounted for the long delay.
9. Online sales account for the majority of our revenue.
10. She couldn't account for the sudden change in his behavior.

Hold someone to account (To hold someone responsible).
It means to force someone to take responsibility and give a clear explanation when things go wrong, emphasizing a strong sense of commitment and rules
Example:
1. I will hold you to account if the kitchen is still a mess tonight.
2. We need to hold the planner to account for the delay.
3. The public will hold the government to account for this policy.
4. We must hold the manager to account for this costly mistake.
5. It's hard to hold them to account without solid evidence.
6. You should hold yourself to account before blaming others.
7. The media plays a role in holding politicians to account.
8. Who will be held to account for this environmental disaster?
9. He was finally held to account for his past actions.
10. The committee will hold the CEO to account at the next meeting.

Take into account (To consider / To factor in).
Instead of just a subjective thought, it means to carefully and objectively factor every small detail into your decision-making formula, showing that you are reliable and thorough
Example:
1. I took everyone's taste into account.
2. You must take the location into account.
3. Did you take the tax and shipping fees into account?
4. We need to take her lack of experience into account.
5. Taking everything into account, I think we did a good job.
6. You have to take inflation into account when investing.
7. I hope the manager will take my request into account.
8. The new design takes the needs of the elderly into account.
9. He didn't take the traffic into account and was late for the interview.
10. We will take your valuable feedback into account for future updates.

Call into account (To question / To challenge the validity of something).
It means to rationally question or challenge something that seems unreliable by logically checking the facts item by item, rather than just arguing.
Example:
1. Your punctuality has been called into account.
2. The ads claim should be called into account.
3. His leadership was called into account after the project failed.
4. The accuracy of the financial report has been called into account.
5. I have to call your hasty decision into account.
6. The witness's statement was called into account by the defense lawyer.
7. We must call these outdated company rules into account.
8. Her true motives were called into account by the committee.
9. The safety of the new product was called into account.
10. It's time to call our current marketing strategy into account.

By all accounts (According to what everyone says).
It means stating a generally recognized fact based on what everyone says, as if everyone's records perfectly match.
Example:
1. By all accounts he is a very nice guy.
2. By all accounts it's a great movie.
3. By all accounts, the year-end party was a huge success.
4. It is, by all accounts, a very difficult exam to pass.
5. She is a brilliant software engineer by all accounts.
6. By all accounts, the business trip to London was a disaster.
7. He is, by all accounts, a fair and reasonable boss.
8. By all accounts, the food at that new restaurant is amazing.
9. The live performance was, by all accounts, excellent.
10. By all accounts, they are very happily married.

Of great account (Of great importance or value).
It means someone or something is extremely valuable and important, acting as an indispensable asset in the overall picture.
Example:
1. He is a person of great account here.
2. His advice is of great account to me.
3. Your opinion is of great account in this matter.
4. The scientific discovery is of great account to humanity.
5. Experience is of great account when applying for this senior role.
6. Family is of great account to her.
7. This environmental issue is of great account to our community.
8. The small details are of great account in this design project.
9. Trust is of great account in any successful relationship.
10. Time is of great account right now, so please hurry.

Of no account (Of no importance / Irrelevant).
It means something is completely unimportant and doesn't even qualify to be recorded in your mind, showing a high level of unbothered calmness.
Example:
1. Don't worry the cost is of no account.
2. Their gossip is of no account to me.
3. His past mistakes are of no account now.
4. The bad weather is of no account; we will go hiking anyway.
5. Money is of no account to a billionaire like him.
6. Her personal opinion is of no account in this business decision.
7. The 10-minute delay is of no account as long as we arrive safely.
8. To her, the brand name of the clothes is of no account.
9. Personal feelings are of no account in a professional negotiation.
10. The exact time of the meeting is of no account at this moment.

On no account (Under no circumstances / Absolutely not).
It means "absolutely not" under any circumstances, conveying an unquestionable strength and an uncompromising attitude.
Example:
1. On no account should you touch my files.
2. On no account will I tell him the secret.
3. On no account should you open this door during the experiment.
4. On no account must this confidential information be leaked.
5. On no account are visitors allowed in the restricted area.
6. On no account will we accept these unfair terms.
7. On no account should young children be left alone at home.
8. On no account can you leave the building before the fire alarm stops.
9. On no account will I change my mind about resigning.
10. On no account should you skip breakfast before a big exam.

On account of (Because of / Due to).
It is used to state an unchangeable objective fact or reason, rather than making excuses or seeking sympathy.
Example:
1. I was absent on account of illness.
2. The flight was delayed on account of the weather.
3. The baseball game was canceled on account of heavy rain.
4. She decided to retire early on account of her poor health.
5. I couldn't sleep at all last night on account of the loud noise.
6. We were late for the wedding on account of the heavy traffic.
7. The startup failed on account of poor financial management.
8. He was excused from the heavy lifting on account of his age.
9. The post office is closed today on account of the national holiday.
10. I chose this specific hotel on account of its excellent location.

Turn to account (To turn to one's advantage / To profit from).
It means to flip a bad situation or a loss and turn it into a new, profitable asset.
Example:
1. I turned the rainy day to account.
2. You should turn your failure to account.
3. She turned her baking hobby to account and started an online business.
4. We must turn this delay to account by preparing our presentation better.
5. He turned his language skills to account during the European trip.
6. How can we turn this empty space in the office to account?
7. A smart entrepreneur knows how to turn every crisis to account.
8. The factory turned the waste materials to account by recycling them.
9. It's important to turn your past work experiences to account in your new job.
10. He turned the harsh criticism to account and significantly improved his writing.
